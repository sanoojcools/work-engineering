## THE ANSWER IN ONE PAGE


### Governing thought

Enterprises should treat the design of work as an engineering discipline before they scale AI. Intelligence is no longer the scarce input. Verification, coordination, authority and accountability are. Work Engineering is the discipline that makes work machine-readable, verifiable and allocatable. The Work Unit is its primitive.


### Six supporting statements

1. **The constraint has moved.** An organization meets five demands every time it turns intent into an outcome. It must supply intelligence, coordination, verification, authority and accountability. AI changes the supply of exactly one of them. The market now supplies intelligence. The organization still supplies the other four. It supplies them through structures that were designed for a world in which all five arrived together, inside one person. Relieving one constraint exposes the other four.

2. **The enterprise cannot govern what it cannot represent.** Enterprises hold a great deal of data about transactions, systems and communications. They rarely hold a machine-readable representation of the accountable commitments that those transactions serve. A trace tells you what happened. It cannot tell you what was permitted, what should have counted as finished, or who owned the consequence.

3. **Work Engineering is the discipline that closes that gap.** It sits between enterprise intent and execution. It takes the enterprise's own representation of itself as its context. It produces a specification of work that an execution system can consume. It does not try to become the execution system.

4. **The Work Unit is the primitive of Work Engineering.** A Work Unit is an independently accountable commitment to move a defined business object from a stated current condition to a stated desired condition, with explicit context, authority, acceptance criteria and evidence. Everything else that Work Engineering produces is a view of that one record.

5. **Verification, not model capability, should gate autonomy.** A system that can act is not the same as a system that can be trusted to act. The VERDICT rubric scores seven properties of a Work Unit. It converts them into one of six autonomy levels. Four hard gates cap autonomy regardless of the score. Promotion up a level is a human decision. Demotion down a level is automatic.

6. **Once work is explicit, allocation and economics become computable.** Choosing between an employee, an agent, deterministic software and an external provider becomes a decision per unit of work rather than a programme per function. Four costing disciplines separate an honest business case from a flattering one. Applying them to our own first case moved the claimed saving from 95 hours a month to 61.8, and the FTE claim from 0.60 to 0.23.


### The honest qualification

Statement 5 is a hypothesis. It has not been tested. Four operational rules in Part H descend from it. A reader who rejects the hypothesis should reject those four rules with it. Section I5 lists all twenty-nine load-bearing claims with their evidence status, so the untested ones are visible without hunting for them.


### What to do first

Run a Work Unit census on one high-volume operational function that has an authoritative system of record. Allow ninety days. The output is a verified inventory of Work Units, a model of which actor should perform each one, and a profile of what each one costs to do and to check. The gaps it finds between declared policy and actual behaviour justify the exercise on their own, before any agent is deployed. Section J1 specifies how to choose the function.


> **Note on how to read:** Two conventions are used throughout and both are explained in Annexure A. Every load-bearing claim is marked as established, design decision, hypothesis or open question, so a reader can see at a glance how much weight it carries.

## PART A — THE MODEL

### A1. The enterprise representation is a container, not the first step in a chain

- **The container:** Enterprise Ecosystem Representation, made of an Ontology and an Enterprise Graph. The Ontology says what entities, objects, actors, capabilities, resources, policies and states exist, and what they mean. The Enterprise Graph says how they connect and what state they are currently in. Everything else happens inside this container. It is not a stage that completes before the next one begins.

- **The downward spine:** Intent, then Work Engineering, then Execution, then Outcome. This is the only part of the architecture that runs one way.

- **The feedback loops:** Three loops that close different gaps (see A3).


**Exhibit 1. The architecture. A container, a downward spine and three loops**

```
[ Enterprise Ecosystem Representation: Ontology + Enterprise Graph (Container) ]
        | Intent (declared)
        v
  [ Work Engineering ]
        |
        v
  [ Execution: Human + Agent + Deterministic Software + External ]
        |
        v
  [ Outcome ]
  Feedback: Discovery Loop, Outcome Loop, Policy Loop
```

> So what: drawing this as a five-layer waterfall would break three things this thesis depends on. The representation would become a step rather than a container. Execution would have no path back to discovery, when traces are the primary input to discovery. And conformance, which is a comparison between observed behaviour in the Enterprise Graph and declared acceptance in the Work Contract, would have nowhere to sit.


### A2. There are two graphs, and confusing them is the common error

| | Enterprise Graph | Work Graph |

|---|---|---|

| Layer | Enterprise Ecosystem Representation | Work Engineering |

| Question | What is connected to what in the enterprise? | What work depends on what other work? |

| Nodes | Business objects, actors, resources, policies | Work Units |

| Example path | Customer → Contract → Order → Product → Invoice → Payment | Validate order → Commit supply → Resolve shortfall → Establish billing → Resolve receivable exception |

| Edges | Semantic and structural relationships | Sequence, shared object, shared resource, reciprocal |

| Built from | Systems, records, ontology declarations | Work Units plus traces |

| Owned by | Enterprise Representation | This document, Section F3 |


> **Key insight:** The Enterprise Graph represents the enterprise. The Work Graph represents the work. An organization that has built the first and believes it has the second will find that it can trace an invoice through six systems and still cannot say who is accountable for resolving a disputed one. Palantir Foundry Ontology is an Enterprise Graph with governed writeback. Celonis object-centric process mining is discovery over an Enterprise Graph. Neither has a Work Graph, because neither has an accountability node to hang edges on.


### A3. Three feedback loops make the architecture continuous rather than periodic

| Loop | Path | What it closes | Where specified |

|---|---|---|---|

| Discovery | Execution → traces and evidence → Enterprise Representation → work discovery | Gap between what org thinks it does and what it does | E4, D2 |

| Outcome | Outcome → intent | Gap between what org committed to and what it achieved | G5, H7 |

| Policy | Verification failures → Work Engineering → Ontology / Policy | Gap between declared policy and enforceable policy | G4, G6 |


### A4. Beneath Work Engineering sit three faster-moving execution layers

- **Execution Layer 1: Actor Orchestration** – Allocates Work Units to actors (human, agent, deterministic software, external provider) per VERDICT autonomy level.

- **Execution Layer 2: Runtime Enforcement** – Enforces authority, evidence, and acceptance criteria at execution time. Governance by construction.

- **Execution Layer 3: Observability & Trajectory Audit** – Logs agent trajectories, scores them via LLM-as-Judge, flags failures.

## PART B — WHY WORK ENGINEERING IS NEEDED NOW

### B1. Organizations meet five demands at once, and AI changes the supply of exactly one

- **Intelligence:** Understanding what to do

- **Coordination:** Sequencing work across actors

- **Verification:** Checking that work was done correctly

- **Authority:** Permission to act

- **Accountability:** Owning the consequence

AI changes supply of intelligence only. Market supplies intelligence, organization still supplies other four via structures designed when all five arrived together inside one person.


### B2. The bundle is what broke, not any single demand

Historically, job roles bundled all five demands. When intelligence was scarce, bundling was efficient. Now that intelligence is abundant and cheap, the bundle becomes the bottleneck.


### B3. Adding intelligence without redesigning the other four produces more output and less assurance

Deploying agents into legacy workflows increases throughput but degrades verifiability and accountability unless work is re-engineered.


### B4. Every existing representation misses the accountability layer

- BPMN models sequence but not accountability

- Data schemas model state but not commitment

- OKRs model intent but not verifiable work

- Process mining shows what happened, not what should count as finished


### B5. The question this raises is what representation of work stays stable while actors change

Needed: a representation where the unit of work is invariant whether performed by human, agent, or software — accountable, verifiable, allocatable.

## PART C — WORK ENGINEERING

### C1. Work Engineering converts intent into work that a machine can read, a checker can verify and an organization can allocate

Definition: The discipline that sits between enterprise intent and execution, taking Enterprise Ecosystem Representation as context and producing a specification of work that execution systems can consume.


### C2. Work Engineering does four things around one primitive

1. **Discovery:** Find work where it already happens and where it should happen

2. **Definition & Design:** Shape work into accountable, verifiable units

3. **Verification Design:** Define how each unit will be checked

4. **Allocation & Economics:** Decide who should do it and what it costs

Primitive: Work Unit. All four orbit it.


### C3. The five outputs are projections of one record, not five artefacts in a queue

- **Work Unit Inventory:** The authoritative list

- **Work Graph:** Dependencies between units

- **Verification Contracts:** How each unit is checked

- **Allocation Model:** Who does what, at what autonomy

- **Economic Model:** Cost to do and cost to check

> They are not produced sequentially; they are views of the same underlying Work Unit records.


### C4. Work Engineering produces a specification, and execution systems consume it

Work Engineering does NOT execute work. Execution systems (human workflow, agent orchestration, RPA, external BPO) consume the spec. This separation preserves auditability.

## PART D — DISCOVERY: WHERE WORK IS FOUND

### D1. Work is discovered upward from traces and derived downward from intent

- **Upward (Empirical):** From execution traces — logs, emails, tickets, transcripts, ERP events, process mining outputs. Uses process mining, object-centric mining, task mining.

- **Downward (Declared):** From intent — policies, SOPs, OKRs, regulatory requirements, job descriptions, contracts.

Both are needed; neither alone is sufficient.


### D2. Discovery supplies candidates, and it under-samples the work least suited to agents

Trace-based discovery over-represents high-volume, system-recorded work. It under-samples tacit coordination, exception handling, and judgment work — precisely the work where agent failure is costliest. Must correct for sampling bias via interviews, shadowing, and intent derivation.


### D3. The conformance gap is the first thing a census produces that anyone will pay for

Comparing discovered (what happens) vs declared (what should happen) immediately reveals:

- Shadow processes

- Policy violations

- Missing acceptance criteria

- Workarounds that indicate ontology gaps

This gap has standalone value before any automation.

## PART E — THE WORK UNIT: THE PRIMITIVE

### E1. A Work Unit is an independently accountable commitment to change the condition of a business object

> Definition: An independently accountable commitment to move a defined business object from a stated current condition to a stated desired condition, with explicit context, authority, acceptance criteria and evidence.

- **Independently accountable:** One owner, one outcome, no joint accountability

- **Business object:** Customer, Order, Invoice, Claim — must be in Ontology

- **Condition:** State machine — e.g., Order: Draft → Validated → Committed → Fulfilled


### E2. Granularity is set by accountability and verifiability, not by size

- Too large: Multiple owners, unverifiable outcome, conflates decisions

- Too small: Overhead exceeds value, no meaningful state change

- Test: Can a checker with no context determine if it is done? Can one person own failure?


### E3. Eighteen contract attributes are what make a Work Unit machine-readable

| # | Attribute | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ID | Unique identifier |
| 2 | Name | Human-readable label |
| 3 | Business Object | What is changed |
| 4 | Current Condition | Pre-state |
| 5 | Desired Condition | Post-state |
| 6 | Context | When/where this applies |
| 7 | Trigger | What causes it to become eligible |
| 8 | Input | Data required |
| 9 | Authority | Who can authorize execution |
| 10 | Actor Constraints | Who/what can perform |
| 11 | Acceptance Criteria | What counts as done |
| 12 | Evidence Required | What must be produced |
| 13 | Verification Method | How checker verifies |
| 14 | SLA / Timing | When it must complete |
| 15 | Dependencies | Other Work Units it depends on |
| 16 | Failure Semantics | What happens if it fails |
| 17 | Regulatory Register Link | Compliance mapping |
| 18 | Provenance | Observed vs invented |


> All 18 must be present for machine-readability. Missing attributes = human-readable but not machine-enforceable.


### E4. The Work Unit is the only record where discovered and declared must be reconciled

Discovery gives you candidates with partial attributes. Intent gives you declared requirements. The Work Unit record is where they merge. Unreconciled = draft; reconciled + verified = authoritative.


### E5. Variants handle non-uniformity without duplicating the inventory

A Work Unit can have variants (e.g., Resolve Receivable Exception – Domestic vs Cross-Border) that share core contract but differ in context, authority, or verification. Prevents inventory explosion.


### E6. A compliance score without a regulatory register entry is an opinion

Link every regulated Work Unit to a Regulatory Register entry (regulation, clause, control objective). Compliance = traceable, not asserted. Score alone without register = opinion.


### E7. Marking provenance separates what we observed from what we invented

- **Observed:** From traces, systems, interviews

- **Declared:** From policy docs

- **Inferred:** Model-generated candidate

- **Designed:** Newly designed during Work Engineering

Provenance tag prevents hallucinated work from acquiring authority.

## PART F — COMPOSITION, DECOMPOSITION AND THE WORK GRAPH

### F1. Eleven process steps produced sixteen Work Units, and the arithmetic is the method

Case example (Order-to-Cash): 11 steps in legacy SOP decomposed into 16 Work Units because accountability split and verification split required it. Count goes up when hidden coordination work is made explicit.

Method: For each step, ask:

- How many business objects change condition?

- How many accountability owners?

- How many distinct verification methods?

If >1, split.


### F2. Three established literatures settle where to cut, and they conflict

1. **Simon (1962) Near-decomposability:** Cut where interactions are weakest

2. **Thompson (1967) Interdependence:** Pooled → Sequential → Reciprocal. Coordination cost rises.

3. **Parnas (1972) Information Hiding:** Cut where change is likely to be contained

> Conflict: Weak interaction ≠ hidden information. Must balance. Exhibit 5 in PDF maps trade-offs.


### F3. Coupling is where coordination lives, and it is specified but not built

Work Graph edges are of four types:

- **Sequence:** B cannot start until A completes

- **Shared Object:** A and B operate on same business object (contention)

- **Shared Resource:** A and B need same actor/capability

- **Reciprocal:** A needs output of B and B needs output of A (requires iteration protocol)


Work Engineering specifies coupling and acceptance, but does not build runtime coordination — execution layer does.

## PART G — VERIFICATION

### G1. The claim, stated as a hypothesis because that is what it is

> **Hypothesis:** Verification, not model capability, should gate autonomy. Autonomy level should be derived from verifiability properties of the Work Unit, not from agent benchmark scores.

- Status: **Hypothesis** (not established). Four operational rules in H depend on it.


### G2. The Work Unit contract is directly usable as an agent evaluation contract

Attributes 11-13 (Acceptance Criteria, Evidence Required, Verification Method) map directly to agent eval:

- Task definition → Business Object + Desired Condition

- Success criteria → Acceptance Criteria

- Trace required → Evidence Required

- Eval method → Verification Method

This reuse is design decision, per Anthropic (2026) eval framing.


### G3. Seven verification methods, and independence is what makes any of them worth its cost

| Method | What it checks | Independence level |

|---|---|---|

| 1. Deterministic Rule Check | Field formats, math, referential integrity | Full |

| 2. Database Constraint / State Check | Object reached desired condition in system of record | Full |

| 3. Cross-System Reconciliation | Same fact in two systems matches | High |

| 4. Human Spot Check | Sample review by domain expert | Medium (if different person) |

| 5. LLM-as-Judge on Evidence | Checks evidence vs criteria | Low-Medium (shared model) |

| 6. Outcome Delay Check | Did downstream failure occur? | Full but lagged |

| 7. Counterparty Confirmation | External party confirms | Full |


> **Key:** Independence = checker does not share failure modes with actor. LLM checking LLM with same prompt = low independence.


### G4. Promotion is a human decision; demotion is automatic

- **Promotion up autonomy ladder:** Requires human approval, evidence of sustained verification pass rate, review of failure semantics.

- **Demotion down:** Automatic on verification failure rate breach, authority violation, or evidence gap. No human needed to demote. Safety bias toward less autonomy.


### G5. Completion is not outcome, and some outcomes need a different kind of Work Unit

- **Completion:** Work Unit reached desired condition per acceptance criteria.

- **Outcome:** Business result that completion was supposed to contribute to.

Some outcomes (e.g., Customer Retained) are not achievable by a single Work Unit; they require a **Sustaining Work Unit** — ongoing commitment with interval verification.


### G6. Governance by construction works only to the extent the runtime enforces it

A Work Unit contract that says 'requires CFO approval' is only governance if runtime prevents execution without that approval token. Otherwise it's documentation. Execution layer must enforce authority, evidence, and condition checks.

## PART H — ALLOCATION AND ECONOMICS

### H1. VERDICT scores the seven supply properties that determine how far a unit can be trusted

| Letter | Property | Question |

|---|---|---|

| V | Verifiability | How independently can we check it? |

| E | Evidence | How complete is the evidence trail? |

| R | Reversibility | How costly is undo if wrong? |

| D | Determinism | How predictable are inputs/outputs? |

| I | Impact Scope | How many downstream objects affected if wrong? |

| C | Compliance / Authority | How regulated is authority? |

| T | Tacitness | How much unwritten judgment required? |


Scoring: 1-5 per property, anchors in Annexure C. Lower score = harder to trust to automation.


### H2. The derivation converts seven scores into one autonomy level, deterministically

**Six autonomy levels:**

1. Human Only

2. Human Led, Agent Assists (draft)

3. Agent Led, Human Approves (HITL gate)

4. Agent Autonomous, Human Reviews Async (Human on the Loop)

5. Agent Autonomous, Exception Only

6. Fully Deterministic Automation (no LLM)


**Four hard gates cap autonomy regardless of VERDICT score:**

- Gate 1: Regulatory — If Compliance score =1 or register requires licensed human, max = L2

- Gate 2: Reversibility — If Reversibility =1 (irreversible financial/legal), max = L3

- Gate 3: Impact — If Impact Scope =1 (enterprise-wide), max = L3

- Gate 4: Evidence — If no evidence path exists, max = L2


Derivation is deterministic and auditable (not LLM-judged).


### H3. Make, agent, automate or buy becomes a decision per unit rather than a programme per function

Once Work Units are explicit with VERDICT and cost:

- Make = employee

- Agent = internal AI agent

- Automate = deterministic software / RPA

- Buy = external provider / BPO

Per-unit decision replaces function-level transformation programmes.


### H4. Accountability boundaries and execution boundaries are different, and conflating them cost us a redesign

- **Accountability boundary:** Who owns outcome if unit fails — stays with human owner even if agent executes.

- **Execution boundary:** Who/what performs the work.

First version conflated them → redesign when agent executed but human accountability unclear. Now separate attributes: Owner vs Actor.


### H5. Four costing disciplines separate an honest business case from a flattering one

1. **Include cost to verify:** Not just cost to do. Verification often 30-60% of total.

2. **Include cost to manage exceptions:** Failure rate * cost to resolve.

3. **Include cost to maintain ontology:** Keeping Work Unit contracts current.

4. **Attribution credibility:** Only count savings where you can attribute hours directly to eliminated Work Units, not self-reported estimates.


### H6. Applying the disciplines moved our own case from 95 hours saved to 61.8

- Initial claim (pre-discipline): 95 hrs/month, 0.60 FTE

- After discipline 1 (verify cost): 78.2 hrs

- After discipline 2 (exceptions): 69.5 hrs

- After discipline 3 (ontology maintenance): 61.8 hrs

- After discipline 4 (attribution filter): FTE claim 0.60 → 0.23 (because many hours were fragmented across roles)

> Honest qualification included in PDF.


### H7. Cost per verified unit works only where attribution is credible

Metric = (Cost to do + Cost to verify + Cost to handle failures) / Verified completions. Usable only where system of record gives authoritative count of verified completions. Avoid where attribution relies on self-report.


### H8. Readability and autonomy are independent, and the dangerous quadrant is already occupied

| | Low Autonomy | High Autonomy |

|---|---|---|

| **High Readability** | Safe, auditable | **High leverage, safe** (goal) |

| **Low Readability** | Toil (human does unreadable work) | **Dangerous quadrant:** Agent does work no one can read/verify — already occupied by many current agent deployments |

## PART I — THE POSITION IS COMPLEMENTARY TO PRIOR ART AND HONEST ABOUT WHAT IS UNPROVEN

### I1. Ten traditions model parts of the problem, and none owns the accountability layer

1. **BPMN / Workflow:** Sequence

2. **Organization Design (Galbraith, Thompson):** Coordination mechanisms

3. **Software Modularity (Parnas, Baldwin & Clark):** Information hiding

4. **Transaction Cost Economics (Coase, Williamson):** Make vs buy

5. **Time-Driven ABC (Kaplan & Anderson):** Costing

6. **Team Topologies (Skelton & Pais):** Team interaction modes, cognitive load

7. **Promise Theory (Burgess):** Autonomous agents, intent, cooperation

8. **Process Mining (van der Aalst):** Discovery from event logs

9. **Object-Centric Process Mining (Celonis):** Enterprise Graph discovery

10. **Ontology / Foundry (Palantir):** Enterprise Graph with writeback


Gap: None has Work Unit as accountable, verifiable primitive + Work Graph.


### I2. Eight established findings we should stop claiming as ours

Document lists eight, including: near-decomposability, pooled/sequential/reciprocal interdependence, information hiding, make-or-buy, TDABC, cognitive load as constraint, process discovery from logs, promises as autonomous agent primitives.


### I3. The favourable evidence is one practitioner-reported case

## PART K — SCOUT: THE CAPTURE INSTRUMENT ACTUALLY BUILT

**A note on how this Part came to exist.** Parts A–J are the original Version 8 concept document. Scout — the interview-based capture instrument that performs Part D's discovery function in practice — was designed and built afterward, in a separate but disciplined effort with its own dated planning documents (`Scout-Reference.md`, `Scout-Enhanced-Vision.md`, `Scout-State.md`, `SCOUT_AMBITIOUS_PLAN.md`, `SCOUT_OPERATING_PLAYBOOK.md`) and its own honesty ledger (`docs/HONESTY.md`, `docs/API.md`). It is placed **after Part J, not interleaved after Part D** where it belongs pedagogically. This is a deliberate, non-cosmetic choice: Parts E through J are cross-referenced by letter — by this document itself (A2, A3, D1, E3, E7, F3, G3, G4, G6, H2, and others), and independently by the built system's own documentation (`ARCHITECTURE.md`, `docs/HONESTY.md`, `SCOUT_OPERATING_PLAYBOOK.md` all cite these letters directly). Inserting a lettered part in the middle would force renumbering every part after it and silently break every one of those external citations. **Evidence tags for this Part.** Parts A–J tag claims `Established | Design Decision | Hypothesis | Open Question` — the register for a paper proposing something. This Part is a record of what was built, not a proposal, so it uses the register Part J4 already introduced for exactly this purpose: **Built** (a real, exercised code path proves it — cited to the router, service, or test that proves it), **Specified, not built** (named in a planning document; no implementation exists), **Design decision** (built, but the specific shape taken was a choice, not dictated by the original design), and **Superseded** (a planning document's own claim about status has since become false — stated in both directions, since some claims moved from "not built" to "built" and it matters which).

Two build efforts since then (the file-upload/classifier work of Slice 1, and Scout Elevated V2's interview capture, PR1 and PR2–4) changed that picture substantially, in ways that do not line up cleanly with the original seven stages — a real capability now exists for most of them, but rarely shaped exactly as specified.

### K2. Scout performs Discovery's downward arm through structured interviews; it does not perform the upward arm

Part D1 names two directions for discovery: upward, empirically, from execution traces (logs, tickets, ERP events); and downward, from declared intent (policy, SOPs, job descriptions). Scout is built entirely as an instrument for the second — a consent-scoped, structured interview that captures a subject-matter expert's or founder's own declared account of their work, one session at a time. No trace-mining, log-ingestion, or ERP-connector code exists anywhere in the system Scout is part of; D1's upward arm remains, for this build, unimplemented regardless of what Scout does. This is a scope statement, not a shortfall relative to what Scout itself was ever asked to do.

### K3. Interview tracks capture the same primitive from different vantage points, and their disagreement is itself a signal

A session was originally typed `founder` or `sme` at creation; K12 renames the first to `function_head` and adds a third, `sub_function_lead`, between them (e.g. Head of TA, Head of People Ops), matching the enterprise persona the product actually targets. All three tracks write into the same Work Capture Grid and are held to the same completeness measure (K5) — the distinction is whose account is being captured, not a different data shape. This design decision is what makes K6's Contradiction Resolver possible at all: because the same underlying work can be described independently by a function head and by the person who actually does it, a disagreement between the two sessions on the same named unit's systems, frequency, or inputs is real, structured signal about where declared intent and lived practice diverge — the same conformance-gap idea Part D3 names, operating between two *declared* accounts rather than between declared and observed. The Contradiction Resolver stays a deliberate 2-way comparison (function_head vs sme) — `sub_function_lead` sessions are not compared against anything yet, a scoping choice K12 states rather than silently leaves implied.

### K4. The Work Capture Grid funnels toward the eighteen-attribute contract without filling all eighteen

A captured unit carries nine fields: `name` (required) and eight optional ones — `inputs`, `outputs`, `systems`, `frequency`, `time_minutes`, `pain`, `handoffs`, `decision_rule`. This is a real, live-editable, inline-updatable record per unit — not a transcript to be parsed later. It is honestly a smaller set than Part E3's eighteen: several required contract attributes the grid never asks for at all — `trigger`, `actor_constraints`, `acceptance_criteria`, `evidence_required`, `failure_semantics` — receive one literal, honest placeholder string when a session is mapped forward into a genome (K7), rather than fabricated content inferred from what was captured. **Design decision**, stated in the mapping code's own documentation, and consistent with Part E7's provenance discipline: a placeholder that says plainly "not captured by this instrument" is not the same claim as a guessed value that happens to look plausible.

### K5. Genome Strength measures what a session has captured, not what a genome needs to pass the gate

Completeness ("Genome Strength" in the interview UI) is a distinct measure from both E3's machine-readability test and from GQS (K8) — it exists to tell an interviewer, live, how close *this session* is to a usable capture, not whether the resulting genome will clear the import gate. It has eight dimensions, not the eighteen contract attributes: one is "Work Units Captured," measured against a published constant (`EXPECTED_UNITS_PER_SESSION = 8`, in the same spirit as the automation-index's own bus-factor constants — a stated threshold, not one invented ad hoc for this document); six more are each grid field's per-unit fill rate; the eighth, "Knowledge Artifacts" (policies, job descriptions, or papers linked to the session), has no real backing field yet and is honestly reported `computed: false` rather than a fabricated `0%` — the overall percentage averages only the seven dimensions that are actually computed. **This is the original design doc's own formula, corrected.** The doc specified completeness as captured-over-expected, with "expected" derived from a job description or execution-log baseline; no such ingestion exists, so the published constant substitutes for it, documented as exactly that substitution rather than silently presented as the original formula.

### K6. Five elevations turn one session into five analytical views, each honest about its own mechanism

Each of the five "elevations" over a session's captured data has a real, specific mechanism — never a placeholder, and never inflated beyond what it actually does:

| Elevation | Real mechanism | Explicitly not claimed |
|---|---|---|
| Time-Travel Replay | Deterministic 09:00–18:00 scheduling arithmetic: units sorted by daily minutes, packed into the window; `frequency` text converts to a daily-minutes figure by a stated rule (day as-is, week ÷5, month ÷22) | Not a prediction or a simulation — a packing calculation over what was already captured |
| Contradiction Resolver | Plain text diff between a function_head-session unit and an SME-session unit sharing a name, across three fields (systems, frequency, inputs); `confidence` is fixed at 1.0 by construction | Not a measured confidence statistic — the field name is honest about what it computes, not what it might imply |
| Pain & Automation X-Ray | A fixed keyword-weight lookup over the free-text `pain` field, capped at five matches; automation-potential is each system's share of total captured daily minutes | Not sentiment analysis, and not a modeled estimate of automatability |
| Story to Structure | With an LLM configured: real extraction, with every returned span checked against the source transcript and discarded if it is not a literal substring. Without one: a deterministic sentence-splitter, and the response states plainly which path ran (`used_llm`) | The model's structured-field readings are labelled as a reading, not a quote; a field the transcript does not cover returns empty, never guessed |
| Future Preview | Pure derivation from the other four elevations' output; unlocks at `completeness_pct >= 100`; reuses the pain-heatmap total for its time-saved figure rather than a separate estimate | No new inference of its own — and, by deliberate choice stated in the UI copy itself, no confetti or other completion flourish the original design called for, to match the product's existing minimal-motion style |

### K7. Generating Work Units from a session reuses the existing import pipeline and faces the same gate without exception

"Generate V8 Work Units" (Future Preview's payoff) calls the same `import_genome` orchestration any other genome import uses — there is no second, Scout-specific writer. This is a **design decision** worth stating as plainly as the rest of this document states its own: Scout does not get a relaxed gate for being the product's own capture instrument. A thin, fully-complete-by-Genome-Strength session predictably scores low under GQS and is correctly blocked — verified live at an eight-unit, 100%-Genome-Strength session scoring GQS 30, with no gates passed — because K4's placeholder attributes and K8's honest provenance labeling both weigh against it structurally. The product's own demo narrative states this outcome as the intended one, not a shortcoming to route around: Scout gets a structured, honestly-labelled draft to the gate; the gate is what tells you whether it is document-backed enough to trust.

### K8. Provenance is honestly declared, not observed — with a direct, structural consequence under this implementation's own quality gate

Every Work Unit a Scout session produces carries `provenance.source_type: "declared"` under Part E7's own four-way taxonomy (observed / declared / inferred / designed) — an interview account is, honestly, a declared account, not an observed one, whatever its accuracy. This implementation's own quality gate (GQS — introduced in the planning docs that sit alongside this one, not itself part of the original V8 text; it operationalizes E3's machine-readability test and E7's provenance distinction into a single pre-write score) weights the `observed` share of a genome at 40% of the total score. The consequence is structural, not incidental: **any Scout-only genome is capped well under the gate threshold by construction**, regardless of how complete or well-captured the underlying session is. This is the single most important thing to understand about what Scout produces versus what a document-backed import produces, and it is presented in the product's own documentation as a finding to state plainly, not a defect to quietly work around.

### K9. Consent is now a real, auditable, revocable object — and, for the live Scout path, now load-bearing

A session may optionally cite a `consent_receipt_id` at creation. Consent receipts themselves are real: created, listed, revoked, and swept by an automated purge job that tombstones anything past its stated retention window, all tenant-isolated the same way a Work Unit is. **The join this section originally called the open piece is now built** (enterprise-readiness roadmap, "make the spec trustworthy"): `services/genome_import.py::_validate_consent` runs whenever a caller opts in via `enforce_consent=True`, and `services/scout_genome.py::generate_genome` — the one real, live Scout-session-to-genome path — does exactly that. A unit whose `provenance.interview_id` is set (every Scout-generated unit now carries one) must also cite a `consent_receipt_id` that resolves to a real, active receipt for that tenant, or the whole batch is rejected with a `missing_consent`/`invalid_consent` violation, same pre-pass shape as every other import gate. A unit's origin can now be traced back to the consent that authorized it: `WorkUnitProvenanceDetail.consent_receipt_id` is written from the validated value instead of hardcoded `None`.

Deliberately **not** wired into the generic JSON-body import (`POST /api/genome/import`, `enforce_consent` stays `False` there) — `provenance.interview_id`/`consent_receipt_id` are free text any caller can set, and the shipped sample genome carries decorative UUID-shaped values in exactly those fields that predate the real consent-receipt feature and were never meant to resolve against it. Enforcing there would block a well-established fixture over data that was never real consent-tracking to begin with. What remains genuinely open: nothing in the Scout Interview UI prompts a user to create a consent receipt before starting a session — the API requires and checks one at genome-generation time, but no screen guides anyone to `POST /api/consent/receipts` first. A session created today, exactly as the UI walks you through it, still has no receipt and will be blocked the moment "Generate V8 Work Units" is pressed — an honest, working gate with no matching onboarding step yet.

Six of those fifteen have since changed status because of the work this Part documents; the other nine have not, and are not re-litigated here — see that file for the full original table, and `docs/HONESTY.md` for the current status of every gate.

### K11. What was specified and, precisely, still is not built

Stated once, plainly, so nothing here has to be inferred from a table's overall tone:

- **A Function Pack SDK** — a loadable ontology + question bank + regulatory stubs + parser hints + verdict anchors per business function, with a CLI. No `pack.json`, no loader, no `scout pack validate` command exists anywhere in the codebase.
- **Adaptive, AI-generated interview follow-up questions.** `DiscoveryPartner`'s question bank is static and hand-written per track; nothing adjusts a question based on a prior answer.
- **Live "words become structure" extraction as a general interview experience.** Real extraction exists only at the Story to Structure elevation specifically, and only when a model is configured (K6); it is not how the rest of the interview UI behaves.
- **The F1 split rule and a dedup engine.** No code inspects an incoming Work Unit for more than one business object, authority, or verification method and splits it; no cross-file or cross-session dedup key computation exists.
- **A dedicated pain-signals table.** The original design specified one as optional; it was not created — pain is derived on read from the captured units already stored, at no schema cost for skipping it.
- ~~DFS cycle detection on the Work Graph.~~ **Built, after this was written** (enterprise-readiness roadmap, "make the spec trustworthy"): `services/genome_import.py::_detect_dependency_cycles` runs as a pre-pass over every import — Scout-originated or not — and rejects a genome whose `dependencies[]` form a cycle, reporting the actual cycle path. Previously the underlying gate validated only that a dependency reference *exists*, not that the graph it forms is acyclic — a real, reachable gap: the shipped sample genome fixture had exactly this shape once (two work units naming each other as dependencies), fixed by hand before this check existed. See `docs/HONESTY.md`'s "Track 3" entry.
- **Consent as a required, gate-enforced precondition of capture** (K9) — the receipt exists and works; nothing yet requires one.

### K12. Three interview tracks, a function-agnostic selector, and the CHRO blast-radius grid replace the original two-track design

Built after K1–K11 were first written, in response to a reviewed build instruction that asked for three specific, well-scoped additions — evaluated against the running code before any of it was accepted, and two of its own claims corrected rather than followed as given (both stated below, not silently fixed).

- **`InterviewType` grows from two organizational altitudes to three.** `founder` is renamed `function_head` (same meaning — e.g. a CHRO; a Postgres `ALTER TYPE ... RENAME VALUE`, so every existing row's meaning is preserved automatically, no data migration) and a new middle tier, `sub_function_lead` (e.g. Head of TA, Head of People Ops), sits between it and `sme`. All three write into the same Work Capture Grid (K4) and are held to the same completeness measure (K5) — the distinction remains whose account is being captured, not a different data shape, exactly as K3 originally stated for two tracks. Contradiction Resolver (K6) stays a deliberate 2-way comparison (`function_head` vs `sme`); `sub_function_lead` sessions are not yet compared against anything, stated in the product's own UI copy rather than left implied.
- **The CHRO blast-radius grid** (`services/scout_blast_radius.py`, `/scout/blast-radius`) is a published, 44-sub-function, 6-cluster HR catalog a `function_head` uses to scope a census: check what is in scope, name an owner, set a P0/P1 priority, and see a live meter (sub-functions selected, percent of the HR stack, clusters touched). The catalog is a code constant, identical for every tenant — what is tenant-scoped is only which rows a given CHRO has actually touched (`ScoutBlastRadiusSelection`, one sparse row per touched sub-function, RLS-isolated like every other Scout table). **Two corrections to the build instruction this was built from, made explicitly rather than followed as given:** its People Operations cluster listed 10 items under a "(9)" label — the only cluster, and the only place, where the two disagreed with each other or with the instruction's own stated 44-item total — resolved by merging two clearly-overlapping items (Employee Self Service into HR Shared Services & Helpdesk) rather than dropping one arbitrarily; and its per-item "typical owner" (e.g. "Head of TA") and "expected work units (8–12)" are not built at all — invented per-item numbers with no real source, the same discipline K5's `EXPECTED_UNITS_PER_SESSION` already applies to a *published* constant instead. Owner and priority in the shipped grid are fields the CHRO fills in during the session; nothing is pre-suggested.
- **A function-agnostic selector** sits above the grid: HR is active and detailed: the catalog above, in full. Finance, Legal, Sales, and Operations render as honest "coming soon" cards — no catalog, no data, and no backend route exists for any of them; selecting one makes no API call. This is a genuinely open gap stated as one, not a placeholder standing in for hidden work, and matches K11's own discipline for naming what is not built.
- **"Start interview" on an in-scope sub-function card hands off, it does not cascade.** Clicking it navigates to the existing session-create form with the track (`sub_function_lead`) and the assigned owner's name pre-filled via router state — a convenience on top of the same create flow K1's stage 1/3 sessions already use, not a new write path and not an automatic interview queue. The interviewer still presses Start; nothing is created until they do.

> So what, for the whole of Part K: Scout is a real, tested, honestly-labelled instrument for one specific job — turning a structured interview into a draft genome that the same uncompromising gate every other genome faces will evaluate on its own terms. It is not the seven-stage, pack-driven, adaptively-questioning discovery engine the original vision described, and this Part's own tables say exactly where the two diverge rather than letting the space between "a lot got built" and "everything got built" go unstated. K12's additions extend the *how* — three tracks instead of two, a scoping grid ahead of them, a selector honest about being HR-only today — without changing that basic shape: still one instrument, still declared not observed, still held to the same gate.

## CONCLUSION

Work Engineering is not another agent framework. It is the layer beneath. It answers: what work exists, what counts as finished, who owns it, how it will be checked, and who should do it.


Without it, adding intelligence produces more output and less assurance. With it, autonomy becomes computable, verifiable, and economically honest.


The primitive is small: one accountable commitment to move one business object from one condition to another, with evidence. The discipline around it is what makes scale trustworthy.

Scout (Part K) is what turns that primitive into a practical starting point rather than a purely aspirational one: a real, tested instrument for capturing declared work through structured interviews, honestly labelled as declared rather than observed, and held to the same uncompromising gate as any other genome. It is the concrete answer to Part D's downward arm, built after this document and recorded here against exactly what it was specified to do — not a claim that discovery's upward arm, or the pack-driven vision the original design described, are finished.

## ANNEXURE C — VERDICT SCORING ANCHORS

| Property | 1 | 3 | 5 |

|---|---|---|---|

| Verifiability | No independent check possible; only actor can say if done | Human spot check possible | Deterministic rule or DB state check |

| Evidence | No trail | Partial logs | Complete, tamper-evident trail in system of record |

| Reversibility | Irreversible external effect (payment sent, contract filed) | Reversible with cost/effort | Fully reversible / idempotent |

| Determinism | Inputs unstructured, outputs open-ended | Semi-structured | Structured inputs, deterministic outputs |

| Impact Scope | Enterprise-wide / regulatory | Department / customer segment | Single object, contained |

| Compliance | Requires licensed human, personal liability | Requires approval, auditable | No special authority |

| Tacitness | Pure judgment, unwritten | Mix of explicit + tacit | Fully explicit procedure |

## ANNEXURE D — GLOSSARY

- **Work Unit:** Independently accountable commitment to change condition of business object

- **Work Graph:** Dependencies between Work Units

- **Enterprise Graph:** Semantic + structural relationships between business objects, actors, resources, policies, and their current state

- **Ontology:** What entities, objects, actors, capabilities, resources, policies and states exist and what they mean

- **VERDICT:** Rubric scoring 7 supply properties (Verifiability, Evidence, Reversibility, Determinism, Impact, Compliance, Tacitness)

- **Autonomy Level:** L1 Human Only → L6 Fully Deterministic Automation

- **Provenance:** Observed / Declared / Inferred / Designed

- **Conformance Gap:** Difference between discovered and declared work

- **Governance by Construction:** Runtime enforces contract, not just documents it

- **Regulatory Register:** External table of regulations, clauses, control objectives

- **Sustaining Work Unit:** Ongoing commitment requiring interval verification (e.g., customer retention)

- **Rule Debt:** Accumulation of agent rules without governance redesign, grows superlinearly (Hydari & Muzaffar 2026)

- **Scout:** The interview-based instrument that performs Discovery's downward, declared arm (Part K). Not a separate product from Work Engineering — its output feeds the same import pipeline and quality gate any other genome faces.

- **Work Capture Grid:** The live, structured, inline-editable record a Scout session fills — nine fields per unit (K4), a smaller set than the eighteen-attribute contract.

- **Genome Strength:** The Scout interview UI's live completeness measure (K5) — distinct from both the eighteen-attribute machine-readability test (E3) and GQS (below); tells an interviewer how close a session is to a usable capture, not whether the resulting genome will pass the gate.

- **Elevation:** One of five analytical views Scout builds over a session's captured data — Time-Travel Replay, Contradiction Resolver, Pain & Automation X-Ray, Story to Structure, Future Preview (K6). Each has a real, specific, honestly-scoped mechanism.

- **GQS (Genome Quality Score):** This implementation's own pre-write quality gate, introduced in the planning docs alongside this one rather than in the original V8 text — an operationalization of E3 (machine-readability) and E7 (provenance) into a single score a genome must clear before any of its Work Units are written (K8).

- **Consent Receipt:** An auditable, revocable, time-bounded record of a data subject's consent that a Scout session may cite (K9) — real and independently tested, not yet a required or gate-enforced precondition of capture.
