**Q1. What does this function own that, if it broke tomorrow, the business would notice within a week?**

Offer release, plain and simple. If Offer Desk stalls, we don't lose a hire in one day — we lose it over two weeks, quietly, as candidates walk to other offers. The two-hour SLA from salary approval to offer letter exists because that's roughly how long a strong candidate waits before a competing offer starts looking better. The second thing is the dual-employment stop — if that check ever gets skipped, it's not a process miss, it's a compliance exposure with UAN and, depending on the case, a fraud question.

**Q2. Which of those are governed by a regulator, versus just internal policy?**

Dual employment via UAN is the regulated one — that's EPFO data, and offering someone who's still shown as employed elsewhere has real statutory exposure. Everything else — the SLA, the salary grid, the bonus splits — is internal policy. Firm policy, but not something a regulator asks us about directly the way UAN service history is.

For this pass, just HR Operations — Offer Desk specifically, under Rashmi. I'm not opening up Talent Acquisition or Total Rewards for this round; those get their own census later. HR Operations reports through the Head of HR Ops, who owns the desk's SLAs and escalation path.
